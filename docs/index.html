WR–CB Matchup Matrix

Receivers don't all beat man coverage the same way. Burst = separation won at the break. Burn = ground won on the corner at full speed. Cornerbacks leak one or the other. Pick a receiver and a corner, and the report tells you which route family to call.

Does it win?

The metrics only matter if they turn into catches. We matched each throw to its intended receiver (play-by-play text) and looked at what happened.

Separation at release → completion

Targeted cut and vertical routes in man coverage, by WR–CB distance when the ball leaves the QB's hand.

Is it a skill or luck?

Out-of-sample test: profiles fitted on odd games, used to predict every route of the even games (and vice versa).

05

Method & limits

  1. Man coverage only (PFF: Cover-0, Cover-1, 2-Man). Zone coverage distorts distances without a real one-on-one duel.
  2. Who covers whom is inferred, not given: each route runner (WR/TE/RB) is paired with the coverage defender that is mutually closest over the first second after the snap, then kept only if that defender stays the closest for ≥ 70% of frames until the throw (≤ 8 yd on average). We keep the WR vs CB duels.
  3. Field normalized: plays going left are rotated 180° (x, y, direction, orientation), so every offense attacks toward the same end zone.
  4. Cut = direction of travel turns ≥ 40° and the body turns ≥ 25° within 0.8 s, at ≥ 2 yd/s, ≥ 2 yd past the line, with the whole window ending before the throw. Burst = change in WR–CB distance over that window.
  5. Vertical = no cut, ≥ 7 yd deep at the throw, within 30° of straight downfield. Burn = yards per second the WR gains on the CB downfield while running straight at ≥ 5 yd/s (raw distance would shrink as the WR eats an off-corner's cushion, even when he is winning).
  6. Opponent-adjusted profiles: ridge regression route = league mean + WR effect + CB effect, penalty chosen by cross-validation across games. Players need ≥ 15 cut + vertical reps (≥ 5 of each).
  7. The call compares expected edges in route standard deviations: (WR effect + CB effect) / spread of that metric. A noisy CB effect is shrunk toward zero, so it weighs less.
  8. Limits: 8 weeks of data, so individual profiles are scouting signals, not certainties; route families are geometric (no route labels in the data); targets are parsed from play-by-play text.
